## Broadcast or Disappear

Most social networks make people choose between broad performance and disappearing into isolated chats. They flatten relationships into followers, hide distribution behind opaque ranking, trap audiences inside platforms, and extract value from the people who create the network.

This network gives every post a **legible, enforceable distribution policy**: who receives it now, who may encounter it later, how it may travel, how long that policy applies, and how it can earn when it creates value.

> **Post at the right distance. Meet people through context and consent. Own the audience relationship and value you create.**

## The First Reason to Open It

People do not join because they want new graph theory. They join because they need to share something that is too personal for public posting, too expansive for a group chat, and too important to lose inside someone else’s platform.

The first durable habit is not scrolling. It is posting an update, opportunity, or work to the right social distance—and receiving a useful response without unwanted exposure.

## The Product Primitive

Social radius is human language for a policy system. A user can begin with calm defaults:

- **Private thought** — selected audience, no discovery, no forwarding
- **Circle update** — direct network, controlled reshare
- **Community post** — chartered community context
- **Opportunity** — consent-qualified introduction through shared context
- **Public work** — followers/Open network, optional creator commerce
- **Gradual release** — starts small and expands only under selected conditions

Advanced controls exist when wanted, but the normal compose experience does not resemble an aircraft cockpit.

## Signature Experiences

### A candid update

Maya writes about a difficult work decision. She picks **My circle**, sees “12 can see this now,” disables forwarding, and sets a 30-day review date. The system enforces access and distribution; it also clearly acknowledges that recipients can still screenshot or describe what they view.

### An opportunity with context

A tenant organizer posts: “Looking for a bilingual volunteer designer in East LA.” They choose **Opportunity**, limited to people who opted into relevant community discovery. A recipient sees “through your network and shared community context.” No intermediary is named unless everyone necessary has consented.

### Public work with fair economics

A filmmaker releases a short documentary to followers and the Open network as pay-what-you-want, permits educational licensing, disallows AI-training use, and assigns a 15% collaborator split. Their ledger shows gross payment, processing, disclosed platform fee, creator net payable, and collaborator allocation.

## Why This Is Different

| Promise | Practical effect |
|---|---|
| Choose social reach | Post policies make audience, discovery, travel, and time understandable |
| Build real connections | Discovery happens through consent, shared purpose, and bounded contexts |
| Own the relationship | Export your work and your consent-respecting audience relationship without claiming others’ private data |
| Earn on chosen terms | Start with tips, pay-what-you-want, splits, and transparent records; expand only when operations support it |
| Control the feed | Ask why something appears, choose a policy, or turn recommendations off |
| Build institutions | Communities can have charters, steward roles, transparent funds, and appeal paths |

## Independent by Design

The network’s identity, graph, content, policy, communities, payment records, and governance are native and canonical. External platforms are optional adapters for identity linking, import candidates, public-content references, and selected outbound distribution.

A user can receive full core value without connecting an external account. An adapter can fail without destroying a user’s graph, posts, access policy, or records.

## Three Horizons

| Horizon | Promise | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Wedge | Honest private-audience posting and bounded consent-based introductions | Launch |
| Platform | Native graph, programmable reach, controlled feeds, communities, commerce, and portability | 1-3 years |
| Constitution | No engagement maximization, portable participant value, transparent extraction/governance process | Permanent |

## Beachhead

Launch begins with 2-3 dense communities of roughly 150-250 people: organizers and creators with real reasons to coordinate privately, surface opportunities, and connect across shared contexts. The product proves trust and connection before it broadens discovery or commerce.

## Why It Is Hard to Copy Well

The moat is not a visibility selector. It is the integrated system: a native graph with explicit relationship types; consent-qualified bridges that do not leak topology; a calm policy composer; enforceable trust architecture; community operations; portable creator economics; and a constitution that limits extractive incentives.
