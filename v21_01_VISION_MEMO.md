## The Problem

Social networks flatten relationships into followers and flatten posting into public or private. A thought for trusted collaborators, a local opportunity that should travel carefully through a community, and a public work intended for broad reach are handled by systems that obscure audience, optimize for engagement, trap social graphs, and capture most of the value users create.

People therefore either perform into a broad feed, disappear into one-to-one or group messaging, or stitch together a fragile stack of apps. Creators and communities then rent their audience, their reach, and their economic relationship from platforms whose incentives are misaligned with theirs.

## The New Primitive

This network is built around **programmable social reach**: people decide who receives something now, who may discover it later, how it can travel, how long the policy applies, and—when relevant—how it earns.

The public promise is simple:

> **Post to the right distance. Build real relationships. Own your audience relationship. Earn on your terms.**

The system makes reach legible in human terms rather than graph theory. Users see modes such as Private thought, My circle, Community post, Opportunity, Public work, and Gradual release—not raw degrees or permission matrices.

## Three Signature Flows

### Thoughtful personal post

Maya writes about a difficult work decision. She selects **My circle**, sees “12 can see now,” disables forwarding, and sets a 30-day review date. Nobody outside the selected audience can discover it through graph proximity or recommendation.

### Opportunity through the network

A tenant organizer posts: “Looking for a bilingual volunteer designer in East LA.” They choose **Opportunity**, limited to opted-in community bridges. Potential recipients see “through your network,” but neither side sees the intermediary unless that person explicitly agrees to introduce them.

### Paid public work

A filmmaker publishes a short documentary to followers and the Open network. It is pay-what-you-want with a suggested contribution; educational licensing is allowed, AI-training use is not; an editor receives a 15% split. The earnings ledger shows gross payments, processing, platform fee, and collaborator allocation.

## Why People Switch

| Promise | User-facing meaning |
|---|---|
| Choose your social distance | Every post goes exactly as far as you choose |
| Own your audience relationship | Leave with content, consent-respecting relationships, and economic history |
| Earn your way | Choose free, tips, pay-what-you-want, paid work, membership, or collaborative funding over time |
| Better discovery | Meet relevant people through context, consent, and shared purpose—not rage ranking |
| Understand your feed | See why content appears, change the policy, or turn recommendations off |
| Communities with real power | Communities can operate with charters, norms, transparent funds, and bounded governance |
| No engagement tax | The business model and ranking do not depend on making users angry or addicted |

## Three Horizons

| Horizon | Promise | Time horizon |
|---|---|---|
| **Wedge** | Honest private-audience posts and bounded consent-based introductions | Launch |
| **Platform** | Native graph, programmable reach, controlled feeds, communities, commerce, and portable identity | 1-3 years |
| **Constitution** | Portable user value, capped extraction, transparent governance, and no engagement maximization | Permanent |

## Initial Beachhead

The initial users are community organizers and creators with a semi-public presence who need a reliable private layer for collaborators and trusted peers, and who benefit from intentional introductions inside dense communities.

Launch uses 2-3 communities of roughly 150-250 members with shared purpose, working norms, willing operators, and enough overlap to establish density. The product is not launched as a replacement for every app; it is launched as the best place to manage meaningful, context-sensitive social reach.

## Why This Is Hard to Copy Well

A visibility selector alone is easy to imitate. The hard system is the combination of:

- A native graph whose relationship types are explicit and portable
- Consent-qualified bridging without graph-topology leaks
- A coherent, low-friction reach composer rather than a settings maze
- Trustworthy operational safety, deletion, and portability
- Community charters and operator practice
- Creator commerce that supports collaborators and transparent value flow
- A culture and economic constitution that reject engagement-maximizing extraction

## Economic Thesis

Privacy makes candid posting possible. Honest distance makes discovery useful. Trustworthy relationships and portable audiences make durable creator and community economics possible.

Creators and communities choose how value is exchanged; the platform supplies reliable rails, transparent accounting, and a constitutional limit on extraction. The full commitment is in `04_COMMERCE_OWNERSHIP.md`.

## What This Is Not

It is not a more private Bluesky client, a conventional creator storefront, a group-chat replacement, a black-box “people you may know” feed, or a tokenized ownership scheme.

External platforms can help users arrive and distribute selected public work. They do not own the graph, determine the product’s survival, or define the social experience.
