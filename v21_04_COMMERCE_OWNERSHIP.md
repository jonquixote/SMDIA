## Purpose

Define who controls the platform, who receives economic upside, who allocates shared resources, and what the company is constitutionally prohibited from extracting or rewarding.

## Three Distinct Questions

1. **Control:** who governs the company and platform rules?
2. **Economic upside:** who receives revenue, surplus, and contributor compensation?
3. **Resource allocation:** who decides how community/shared funds are spent?

Tips alone answer none of these fully. Revenue sharing answers only part of economic upside. Participatory budgeting addresses allocation. Governance rights require their own rules.

## Direction of Travel

The preferred direction is **steward ownership plus a governed community-benefit pool**, subject to formal legal, tax, payment, securities, and corporate-structure review.

The product rejects token-first, speculative pseudo-ownership. Any participation right must be legible, non-extractive, anti-capture, legally reviewed, and not marketed as an investment return unless it is legally structured as one.

## Creator Commerce Principle

> The creator selects the business model. The platform supplies payment rails, discovery, fulfillment, analytics, and trust—not mandatory extraction.

## Early Commerce Sharp Edge

The launch economic story is intentionally narrow:

> **Earn how you want, split credit fairly, and see exactly where the money went.**

The first coherent stack is tips, pay-what-you-want work, collaborator splits, a creator earnings ledger, and visible community-fund allocation. It is enough to prove creator sovereignty without building an ad network, subscription business, ticketing marketplace, licensing marketplace, and tax platform at once.

## Platform Fee Policy

The platform publishes:

- Base transaction fee and what it covers
- Payment-processing pass-through costs
- Payout timing
- Refund/chargeback handling
- Any fee ceiling or process required to change fees
- Whether different products have different disclosed fee schedules

Fee changes require advance notice, versioned policy history, and any constitutionally required review. The system never hides extraction in opaque ranking, advertising targeting based on private content, or undisclosed payout deductions.

## Value-Flow Waterfall

The ledger reports the following order:

1. Payment processing, chargebacks, taxes, refunds, and direct transaction costs
2. Worker compensation and operating expenses
3. Resilience reserve with published target and release policy
4. Capped, disclosed product/growth reinvestment budget
5. Investor return, if applicable, under a disclosed cap or mission lock
6. Remaining surplus allocated by published rules among creators, contributors/community operators, and the community-benefit pool

The precise percentages, reserve target, investor cap, payout eligibility, allocation formulas, and amendment process are mandatory legal/finance Phase 0 decisions before public economic promises exceed the MVP ledger.

## Payments and Entitlements

The Commerce PRD and implementation must define:

- Payment authorization and payout schedule
- Refunds, chargebacks, disputes, fraud, and tax responsibilities
- Entitlement lifecycle: purchase, access, cancellation, revocation, expiry, refund
- Digital-good fulfillment
- Collaborator split calculation and failure handling
- Creator identity/payout verification
- Portable ledger, invoices, payout statements, licenses, tax records, and contributor history
- Anti-farming controls: no payout formula based on raw likes, outrage, follower count, invite spam, or fabricated transactions

## Community Funds

Community funds are explicit pools with:

- Defined revenue source and budget limits
- Published purpose and eligible uses
- Eligibility and conflict-of-interest rules
- Participatory-budget process where appropriate
- Voting caps and anti-capture protections
- Transparent allocations, receipts, and decision record
- Operator compensation rules

A community fund is not an implied promise of profit sharing.

## Governance Rights

The Phase 0 constitution determines:

- Corporate form and mission lock
- Stakeholders with rights: workers, users, creators, operators, investors, or defined groups
- Constitutional decisions versus operational decisions
- Voting eligibility, caps, delegation, quorum, and appeals
- Member/community roles and anti-capture protections
- Transferability, redeemability, and speculative restrictions on any participation rights
- Amendment procedure and notice requirements

## Portability Commitments

| Asset | Portability promise |
|---|---|
| Your content | Full export of source media, text, metadata, rights, revisions, and commerce records |
| Your audience relationship | Consent-respecting connection record and portable opt-in channel data only where the other person permits it |
| Your community role | Memberships, charter, contributions, decisions, entitlements, and permitted directory data |
| Your earnings | Full ledger, payouts, invoices, splits, licenses, tax documentation, and entitlement history |

**Principle:** you own your relationship to your audience, not other people’s private identity or contact data.

## Transparency and Audit

- Publish value-flow ledger on a defined cadence
- Publish methodology and changes to fee/allocation rules
- Preserve version history for economic policy
- Establish independent or community-readable review/audit process as scale warrants
- Disclose conflicts, reserve changes, and material governance decisions

## Legal Review Questions

- Corporate structure and mission-lock enforceability
- Payment licensing, money-transmission exposure, and processor requirements
- Tax collection/reporting and creator payout obligations
- Securities-law implications of any surplus/revenue/participation mechanism
- Consumer protection, refunds, subscriptions, and digital goods
- Licensing, rights management, AI-use permissions, and contributor contracts
- Privacy/data portability obligations across jurisdictions
