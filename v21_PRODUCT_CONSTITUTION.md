## Product Statement

> Social networks force people to choose between broadcasting and disappearing. This network gives every post a controllable social radius: who can receive it now, how far it can travel, whether it can be reshared, and how it can earn.
>
> People own their content, audience relationship, identity, and economic relationship with supporters. They can import from other networks and distribute to them, but their relationships and value do not belong to those networks.
>
> The product is built to make intentional connection, not maximum engagement, the default.

## Strategic Hierarchy

1. **Programmable social reach** is the core interface invention.
2. **The native graph** is the core strategic asset.
3. **Creator and community economic sovereignty** is the commercial promise.
4. **Privacy and consent** are the trust substrate.
5. **External services are optional adapters**, never the source of truth.

## Canonical System Rule

Native identity, graph, content, visibility policy, community membership, payment entitlement, and governance records are canonical. Imports are accelerants. Cross-posts are outbound copies. An adapter failure degrades convenience, not core operation.

## Canonical Reach Model

\[
\text{Eligible reach} = \text{author intent} \cap \text{recipient bridge consent} \cap \text{context rules} \cap \text{safety thresholds}
\]

Graph distance alone never authorizes distribution. The system separates:

- **Audience:** people who may receive a post now
- **Reach:** people who may encounter it later under policy
- **Distribution:** how content may move (recommendation, reshare, external copy, introduction)
- **Discovery:** whether a person may be offered a connection opportunity
- **Bridge consent:** whether someone may form a consented path between people

## Five Graphs

| Graph | Meaning | Must never imply |
|---|---|---|
| Follow | “I want to see this person” | Private access or bridge consent |
| Private audience | “This person may receive this post” | Future access, discovery, governance, or public reach |
| Interaction | “We actually engaged” | Trust, entitlement, or private access |
| Discovery bridge | “A consented path may support an introduction/discovery” | Endorsement, intermediary disclosure, or private access |
| Economic | “This person created, funded, curated, or stewarded value” | Intimacy, private influence, or engagement-based power |

No graph silently creates an edge in another graph.

## Ferrari Principles

- Native graph first; imports are accelerants, not dependencies.
- Relationship types are explicit.
- Every post has an understandable audience, reach, distribution, time, and economic policy.
- Discovery creates consented opportunity, not engagement-maximizing surveillance.
- Creators and communities choose how value is exchanged.
- Platform extraction is capped, legible, and accountable.
- Portability is real and consent-respecting.
- Privacy is architectural, not a label.
- Competition is won through execution, trust, culture, graph quality, and economic legitimacy—not lock-in.

## Anti-Goals

- No engagement-maximizing ranking objective
- No forced publicness, silent audience expansion, or dark patterns
- No external-network dependency for core product operation
- No opaque payout or ranking calculation
- No sale of private content or relationship data
- No token-first or speculative pseudo-ownership scheme
- No topology exposure, friends-of-friends propagation, or bridge use without policy and consent
- No assumption that every user wants discovery, monetization, or governance

## Protected Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Private audience** | Explicit people authorized to receive a specific private post or preset; not a follow list |
| **Social radius / reach policy** | Author-readable policy for immediate audience, later reach, distribution, duration, and optional economic terms |
| **Bridge consent** | A user’s explicit permission to be a possible context-qualified path for introductions/discovery |
| **Open network** | Native public, searchable network surface; not an external platform |
| **Community** | Chartered social space with defined membership, norms, roles, and governance boundaries |
| **Adapter** | Replaceable integration that can link identity, import candidates, reference public content, export cross-posts, or provide a payment link |
| **Portability** | Export of a user’s own records and consent-respecting relationship metadata; never ownership of another person’s private data |

## Non-Negotiable Invariants

- Private content never reaches an external endpoint.
- Compose never represents an inactive invitee as a current viewer.
- Imported relationships never become private authorization, bridge consent, reputation, governance, or entitlement automatically.
- External writes require explicit destination disclosure and user acknowledgment.
- Graph topology is not exposed through reach estimates, degree displays, or intermediary identification without the required consent and anonymity thresholds.
- Ranking is not authorization.
- Users own their relationship to an audience, not other people’s private data.
- The platform publishes its value-flow commitments and does not reward raw engagement, outrage, follower count, or invite spam.
